01 / Observe · Empirical foundation · Agency · Expression

AI-Assisted Interpersonal Communication

Can AI improve communication without improving the communicator?

Analysis ongoing · Manuscript in preparation

Context
University of Southern California
Prof. Angel Hsing-Chi Hwang · 2026–Present
My contribution
Theoretical framing · Conversational coding
Mixed-methods analysis · Research translation

01 / Question

In conflict, apology, emotional uncertainty, and difficult self-expression, AI can help people decide what to say. Whether the person becomes better able to interpret and respond independently is a separate question.

02 / Invention & Method

Distinguish assisted performance from independent competence. A response can feel more sincere or easier to use while its author feels less ownership. These outcomes should be measured separately, rather than collapsed into a single claim that AI “helps.”

03 / Mechanism

My role

  • Lead theoretical framing and mixed-methods analysis.
  • Develop coding frameworks for prompting strategies, emotional validation, interpersonal planning, and changes in communication strategy.
  • Integrate chatbot conversations, interviews, open-ended responses, and pre–post measures; translate findings into an online guide and follow-up feedback survey.

The study design and data collection are part of the collaborative project with Professor Hwang. The contributions above describe my individual work.

Study and analysis

The documented sequence is pre-survey → chatbot interaction → post-survey, across two pilots and a 222-person main study. I combine theory-driven coding with mixed-effects analyses of pre–post measures. Conversational coding describes prompting, AI functions, and communication strategies; it is not a test of unaided interpersonal competence.

05 / Next Experiment

After AI support is withdrawn, do people use their own judgment, consider another person’s perspective, and communicate differently in a subsequent human interaction? Repeated assessment could distinguish immediate assistance from learning over time.

02 / Build · Interactive research artifact

Scaffolding AI: When the Helper Leaves

Can AI help with a difficult exchange while leaving the reasoning with the person?

Working scripted demonstration · v0.3.0 · Human evaluation proposed

Context
New research artifact developed for this portfolio with Codex assistance
Implemented
Three policies · Seven phases · Two fictional scenario pairs

Question

Does assistance strengthen the person’s independent communication, or perform some of that competence on their behalf?

Original Idea

Turn the distance between assisted performance and independent capability into an inspectable interaction: begin with the person’s own reading, vary the help, then remove it before a new task.

What I Built

A functioning browser demo with Direct AI, Scaffolding AI, and Independent Reflection. It records an initial interpretation, five reflections in scaffold mode, a self-selected communication strategy, an owned response, and a new unaided response. Reset and mode switching clear the previous session.

The behavioral framing follows my ongoing interpersonal-communication work. Implementation developed with Codex assistance; this is not a prior solo-engineering artifact, an approved intervention, or a USC study result.

Try the complete flow ↗

How It Works

Immutable policies and public scenario choices feed a validated seven-phase state machine. The interface renders text safely. Ordered local events record step IDs, strategy IDs, and character counts. The new-task view excludes earlier reasoning, suggestions, drafts, strategy labels, and the trace.

The policy is authored, not generated from your input. Strategy labels are self-reported choices, not NLP classifications. Text stays in page memory; there is no collection endpoint or model call.

Study Design

Proposed: randomize three policy packages, obtain an unaided baseline, and evaluate new-task responses with a prespecified rubric and coders blind to condition. Pilot effort, duration, task difficulty, and exposure before choosing the primary contrast.

The public demo is self-selected and unmatched. Independent Reflection still includes a strategy menu. No-assistance does not mean no instruction or practice.

Evidence

The flow works; learning remains untested.

Software tests cover all modes, validation, strategy routing, phase boundaries, detached local records, and the unaided screen. Browser walkthroughs are documented separately. There are no human pilot results, learning scores, or confirmatory effects.

What Was Learned

The earlier two-mode implementation did not record the initial interpretation or an explicit strategy choice. This version adds both and separates writing the response from guided reflection. Those changes make the manipulation and its limits more inspectable; they do not remove the effort and practice confounds.

Next Version

Validate fictional stimuli and blinded ratings, then add approved random assignment and data management. Only add generative responses if they improve the research manipulation and can be reproduced and evaluated.

03 / Scale · Build + Study · Artificial social actors

From Private AI Relationships to Shared Social Reality

A shared artificial character, independent private encounters, and a testable path into later human cooperation.

Prototype 01 implemented · Scripted browser MVP · Behavioral pilot not started

Research context
University of Glasgow
Dr. Chen Zhou · 2026–Present
Current implementation
Three-turn scripted interaction · Separate page state
Shared/separate identity × immediate/seven-day handoff preview
Try the working prototype ↗Code & README ↗

01 / Question

Can separate people develop relationships with the same artificial social actor, and does that common reference change trust, cooperation, or shared understanding when they first meet?

The current build tests identity/state feasibility. The psychological relationship and transfer remain hypotheses.

Conceptual contribution

The project connects private human–AI experience to later human–human group dynamics. Building on the lab's existing paradigm, my temporal extension asks whether a shared-character effect is detectable at a first meeting seven days later.

The lab developed the broader paradigm; my contribution focuses on the delayed first human meeting.

02 / Invention

Prototype 01 is now a functioning browser implementation: configurable character identity and delay, three-turn personalization, independent session state, ordered event logs, local JSON export, and a facilitator handoff with a payoff explanation.

My research contribution is the behavioral framing, temporal design, and pilot development with Dr. Zhou. The browser implementation was developed with Codex assistance. Its architecture, tests, and development decisions are documented; behavioral validation is the next stage.

03 / Mechanism

A public registry defines continuing identities. Each page holds only its own choices, transcript, and events in memory. Scripted responses branch on those local choices. Shared identity never imports someone else's private conversation.

No LLM, database, or research collection endpoint is connected. Reload/reset clears progress; a downloaded record stays on the visitor's device.

Proposed study design

The proposed experiment crosses shared/separate character identity with immediate/seven-day first human meetings, using independent three-person groups. The AI is absent during the human-only cooperation decision and later discussion.

The public demo reveals controls and simulates delayed handoff. Real scheduling, independent commitments, compensation, ethics approval, and a valid manipulation are still required.

04 / Evidence

Working software ≠ human evidence

The code tests identity mapping, private-state independence, transition rules, timing, exports, and payoff arithmetic. No participant pilot or confirmatory results exist for this implementation.

Technical feasibility cannot establish felt connection, shared reality, cooperation effects, or psychological persistence. The verification report separates completed checks from untested assumptions.

Implementation lesson

A static host and temporary page state are enough to expose the identity/privacy assumption, but cannot support retained seven-day follow-up. A short scripted exchange also leaves relational credibility unresolved. These constraints led to a deliberate separation of software feasibility from behavioral validation.

No human usability feedback or invented failure narrative is reported. Observed defects and design decisions belong in the iteration log.

05 / Next Experiment

First review and pilot the interaction's comprehension, character continuity, warmth, and responsiveness. Then add approved session authorization, storage, real scheduling, and human-group administration. Use a richer script or an LLM only if it serves the validated behavioral manipulation.

Can one common artificial character help people understand one another, or does it merely make them expect similarity? The next study needs to distinguish those explanations.

04 / Social cues · Inference · Measurement

Inferring Intent from Incomplete Signals

Research support on how facial expression, vocal prosody, and other signals inform judgments of sincerity and moral intent across cultures.

Research assistantship · 2024–2025

Context
Social Perception Lab, University of Glasgow
Prof. Rachael Jack · 2024–2025
Scope
Facial, vocal, and text-derived indicators of social judgment

Research Question

How do people use facial, vocal, and multimodal cues to infer sincerity and moral intent, and how can those judgments be measured carefully across cultures?

Why It Matters

Sincerity and intention are inferred rather than directly observed. Studying the signals and measurements behind those inferences provides a foundation for asking what changes when AI helps generate or interpret social expression.

My Role

  • Support research on facial expression, vocal prosody, and multimodal signals of sincerity and moral intent.
  • Integrate reaction-time, facial EMG, prosodic, and NLP-derived text features in reproducible R workflows.
  • Contribute to preregistration materials, analysis scripts, and preparation for the Society for Affective Science conference in 2025.

These contributions were made as a research assistant in Professor Jack's lab. I distinguish this role from independently designing or leading the lab's studies.

Study at a Glance

Stage & scope
Facial, vocal, and text-derived indicators of social judgment
What I built or contributed
Contributions to reproducible R analysis scripts, multimodal data workflows, preregistration materials, and conference preparation.

The lab work concerns cross-cultural social judgments using facial, vocal, and multimodal information. My analysis work drew on behavioral and text-derived measures, including reaction time, facial EMG, and prosodic features.

Methods & Analysis

Reproducible R analysis connects observable features and behavioral responses to latent social constructs. Preregistration and analysis documentation help make the reasoning behind those connections explicit.

Key Findings

Methods-focused case study

The contribution presented here is the integration of multimodal indicators and research materials. This overview does not report a new study-level effect or a publication led by me.

The schematic summarizes the analytical logic of the work; it is not a diagram of one specific experimental protocol.

What This Made Me Ask

I learned to translate social constructs into measurable indicators without assuming that one signal captures the whole construct. This remains central to how I study sincerity, ownership, and judgment in AI-assisted communication.

Examine how AI-generated or AI-interpreted expression changes the cues people use to judge sincerity, intention, and authorship, and whether those judgments differ across contexts and cultures.

Research Artifacts

05 / Digital behavior · Evidence · Heterogeneity

Gaming Disorder: What Does an Average Effect Hide?

Across 69 eligible meta-analyses, I examine evidence about prevalence, psychosocial correlates, and interventions—and the differences an average can obscure.

Manuscript under review

Context
CARE Lab, University of Hong Kong
Prof. Cecilia Cheng · 2025–May 2026
Scope
Approximately 400 meta-analyses screened · 69 eligible meta-analyses

Research Question

What does meta-analytic evidence tell us about gaming disorder, and how does its interpretation depend on populations, measures, and study designs?

Why It Matters

Digital behavior is often described through a single average effect. Evidence synthesis makes it possible to examine the consistency of that evidence and the differences hidden within pooled estimates.

My Role

  • Screen, synthesize, and code approximately 400 meta-analyses; contribute to the synthesis of 69 eligible meta-analyses.
  • Extract effect sizes, study characteristics, methodological features, and intervention outcomes.
  • Contribute to theoretical interpretation of prevalence patterns, psychosocial correlates, and intervention effects; co-author the manuscript.

The umbrella review is a team project led by Professor Cheng. The 69 eligible meta-analyses describe the review as a whole, not a separate dataset that I alone produced.

Study at a Glance

Stage & scope
Approximately 400 meta-analyses screened · 69 eligible meta-analyses
What I built or contributed
Contributions to structured review-level coding and effect-size extraction, and a co-authored umbrella-review manuscript.

The unit of synthesis is the meta-analysis. The review covers gaming disorder prevalence, psychosocial correlates, and intervention outcomes across 69 eligible meta-analyses.

Methods & Analysis

Systematic screening, effect-size extraction, and methodological coding support comparisons across review-level evidence. I worked with differences in populations, instruments, and designs rather than treating all estimates as interchangeable.

Key Findings

Evidence-synthesis scope

The completed synthesis brings together 69 eligible meta-analyses across three outcome domains. This case study presents the review's scope and my methodological contributions.

Pooled numerical estimates are not reproduced in this portfolio. The manuscript is under review at Nature Human Behaviour.

What This Made Me Ask

This work made me more attentive to the distance between an association and an explanation. For my AI research, I now ask whose experience changes, under which conditions, and what a study design can actually establish.

Bring the same attention to heterogeneity into human–AI research: distinguish populations, contexts, and measures, and use temporal or experimental evidence to test explanations that correlations alone cannot resolve.
