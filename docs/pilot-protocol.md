# Shared Artificial Social Actor — draft Build + Study protocol

**Status:** proposed protocol for supervisor review. No participant pilot or confirmatory experiment has been conducted using Prototype 01. No ethics approval or preregistration for this new implementation is claimed.

## 1. Separate the two questions

**Software feasibility:** can continuing identity and individualized responses coexist without sharing private conversation? Prototype 01 and its software tests address this.

**Behavioral question:** do private encounters with one common artificial social actor change subsequent human–human cooperation and shared understanding? This requires recruited people, a credible manipulation, independent groups, and a study-ready system.

## 2. First pilot: establish a credible manipulation

Before recruitment, obtain institutional ethics approval and supervisor agreement on the script/system, consent, privacy, compensation, and data-management plan. Recruit adults without prior acquaintance within each group; prespecify acquaintance checks and eligibility. No sensitive personal disclosure is needed for the current low-stakes scenario.

Start with usability and manipulation feasibility, not an underpowered efficacy claim. Determine the pilot size and stopping rule before recruitment. There are currently **zero recruited pilot participants** and no frozen sample-size claim.

Evaluate: comprehension of private versus shared identity; character recognition; continuity and distinguishability of the character; felt connection; perceived responsiveness, warmth, usefulness, and response quality; technical completion/errors; and confusion about privacy or timing. Ask participants what they thought was shared. Fixed-choice dialogue may be too brief or too task-focused to induce relational experience; that is an empirical risk, not a feature established by the demo.

Pilot criteria should be decided before data collection: acceptable comprehension/error rates, sufficient recognition, and a defensible quality match between conditions. If the script does not produce the intended experience, revise and rerun feasibility rather than interpreting a cooperation null as evidence against the theory.

## 3. Main design under consideration

Independent three-person groups, randomized at group level to a 2 × 2 design:

| Condition | Artificial character identity | First human meeting |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | Shared continuing character | Immediate |
| B | Separate characters | Immediate |
| C | Shared continuing character | Seven days later |
| D | Separate characters | Seven days later |

Each person interacts separately. The AI is absent during the human meeting. Delayed groups do not meet each other immediately and then reunite; day seven is their **first** human meeting. Equal exposure length/content opportunities, a versioned response policy, and balanced character cues are required. Prevent further exposure before the delayed meeting or document it according to a prespecified policy. Prototype 01's early handoff button is a software clock simulation and must not be used as evidence of an actual seven-day interval.

**Operational definition of shared character:** one continuing social identity with the same name/persona presentation. It is not merely the same model or product. Private content is not jointly seen or shared with partners.

The core question extends an existing lab paradigm; Xuechu's described focus is the temporal comparison. Public portfolio material does not disclose confidential lab slides, unpublished results, or unapproved protocols.

## 4. Hypotheses and comparisons

- **Primary H1:** at the seven-day first meeting, shared-character groups have higher group-average initial contributions than separate-character groups (C − D). This is a proposed directional hypothesis, not a finding.
- **Temporal question:** does the shared-character effect differ between immediate and delayed first meetings? Test `(C − D) − (A − B)` directly, two-sided unless a directional temporal hypothesis is justified in advance.
- **Secondary questions:** do partner-specific shared-reality reports and conversational behaviors differ? These outcomes may diverge from cooperation.

Prespecify the two-test hierarchy or multiplicity adjustment, alpha, covariates, exclusion/missing-group rules, and exploratory analyses before confirmatory recruitment. Do not treat “significant immediately, non-significant later” as evidence of decay.

## 5. Sequence and measures

1. Consent and eligibility; randomized group condition; independent private interactions.
2. Immediate or actual seven-day first meeting; standardized brief neutral introductions.
3. **Primary:** each participant privately commits 0–10 tokens in the first public-goods decision. Multiply the total by 1.5 and divide equally across three members; retain unspent tokens. Check comprehension and implement actual compensation if using this incentive rule. Average the three contributions for the group-level outcome. The public demo calculator is not a substitute for this independently committed decision.
4. A 12-minute human-only discussion about allocating a small newcomer welcome budget. Use the same prompt across conditions; the shared character must not supply a group consensus. Pilot topic familiarity and engagement. This discussion occurs **after** the primary decision, so it cannot be claimed to explain that earlier decision causally.
5. **Secondary:** shared reality with each specified human partner; questions asked, uptake of another person's idea, expressed disagreement, and optional partner-specific trust reports. Adapt established instruments cautiously, pilot wording, specify scoring, and assess reliability. Use blinded conversational coders where feasible and report inter-rater reliability.
6. **Manipulation/process:** character recognition, felt connection, expected similarity to partners, and delayed identity recall. Plan the timing of these questions to minimize priming; piloting them before the primary task can itself change behavior.

The primary outcome operationalizes one instance of cooperation. It does not directly establish friendship, sustained cohesion, trust, or community-level norms. Any norm-formation claim needs a separate operational definition and repeated group observations.

## 6. Analysis and power

Plan sample size in **independent groups**, not isolated participants. Use pilot variance, anticipated attrition/incomplete groups, a justified smallest meaningful contribution difference, and simulation/analytic power for C − D and the identity × delay interaction. Freeze the sample size and stopping rule before confirmatory recruitment; do not assume this demonstration supplies an effect size.

Analyze first-decision group means with the prespecified factorial model/contrasts; report effects in tokens and confidence intervals. Account for group/partner dependence in secondary reports and conversational outcomes. Report condition-specific attrition and incomplete-group handling. Keep intention-to-treat interpretation explicit; do not automatically exclude forgetful people or adjust for post-treatment connection/recall.

If making a stability or meaningful-null claim, prespecify an equivalence margin and an appropriately powered equivalence analysis. Two time points cannot establish a continuous effect or its exact duration. Measures collected after cooperation cannot establish mediation of the earlier behavioral outcome.

## 7. Competing explanations and next decisive tests

Common labels, shared task experience, exposure content, expected similarity, character familiarity, and response quality can all contribute. Name rotation helps control specific identities but does not prove the “shared relationship” mechanism. In the scripted MVP, identity cues are intentionally varied while response policies are matched; a resulting effect would not alone isolate relational connection.

After feasibility and an independent replication, select one informative extension: shared/separate identity × relational/task-focused interaction, or × convergent/divergent interpretation support, all at the delayed first meeting. Each interaction tests a specific package/boundary, not causal mediation by itself. Recruit independently unless cohort reuse and carryover handling were planned in advance.

## 8. Ethics and community relevance

Consent must disclose AI involvement accurately; separate identifiable recruitment records from interaction/behavioral data; approve storage, access, retention, and withdrawal procedures; avoid unnecessary intimate disclosures; and keep group members' private conversations private. If recording a group, obtain separate recording consent and state what is shared with researchers. Compensation must not depend on socially desirable choices.

The intended contribution is to understand whether a common artificial social reference supports or distorts later human coordination and connection. Human flourishing is a motivation to test benefits and costs, not an outcome already demonstrated by this prototype.
