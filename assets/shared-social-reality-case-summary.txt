From Private AI Relationships to Shared Social Reality
University of Glasgow
Experimental protocol development · Pilot preparation

Portfolio case summary; not a full manuscript.

Research Question
Can separate relationships with the same artificial social actor create a shared social reality—and change subsequent trust, cooperation, and group dynamics?

Why It Matters
An artificial actor may become a common social reference even when people encounter it separately. Studying the later human meeting asks whether AI experience carries outward into human social life.

Study at a Glance
The proposed design crosses shared versus separate virtual-character identities with an immediate versus seven-day-delayed first human meeting. Participants interact privately with the character beforehand; the AI is absent during the subsequent human group interaction.

What I Built or Contributed
An experimental framework and temporal-comparison pilot plan, both in development.

Methods & Analysis
The protocol pairs a behavioral cooperation task with reports of trust and shared understanding. Group-level analysis and a direct identity-by-delay comparison are planned. Sample size and procedures are being refined before the pilot.

Key Findings / Current Evidence
The current output is an experimental framework and pilot plan. The shared-character effect and its temporal persistence are questions for the planned experiment.

Interpretation
No experimental results or implemented virtual-agent prototype are claimed in this case study.

What This Made Me Ask
Protocol development made me distinguish a shared character from a shared conversation, and a delayed first meeting from following up a group that has already met.

Next Research Question
If a common artificial character changes how people first meet, is that difference still detectable seven days later? What separates shared understanding from a common label or an expectation of similarity—and how might group norms emerge?

Research Artifacts
Experimental framework and pilot procedures in development. The schematic is an explanatory artifact; it does not depict an implemented system or observed results.
